The Future of Faith Off Earth

When people leave this planet and stay, the question of worship does not disappear. Someone has to prepare for it.

Church of Mars is a non-denominational Christian effort to do that work now. We are not waiting for the first permanent habitat to ask what a house of worship looks like on another world. We are starting the conversation.

The white cross stands in contrast against the iron-oxide landscape for a reason. Representation matters, even before the first permanent boots are on the ground.

A project of Milky Way Ministries.

Conceptual Structure

A compact barrel-vaulted nave, thick walls of 3D-printed and sintered Martian regolith for radiation shielding and thermal mass, partially bermed, with a high-albedo white cross that remains clean and visible against the red terrain.

ISRU Construction

Walls 2.5–3.5 meters thick of robotic 3D-printed sulfur-concrete or sintered regolith. Layered print texture matches the surrounding soil. Airlock entrance. Pressurized interior with liner.

The White Cross

High-contrast pure white geometric cross mounted on the facade. High-albedo material that stands out sharply against the rusty landscape and hazy sky. Visible from a distance.

Phased Roadmap

1. Symbolic

Bible, communion elements, or microfilm on an early uncrewed cargo flight. Low mass. First Christian representation on the surface.

2. Modular

Habitat-integrated or standalone regolith chapel with the first crews. Radiation-shielded, pressurized, functional for a small congregation.

3. Congregation

Self-sustaining local church once permanent settlement exists. Built from the ground up with local materials and people who choose to stay.

The First Congregation Starts on Earth

Before any boots are on Martian soil, the conversation has to exist here. Church of Mars is that conversation — non-denominational, low-shame, and clear that people living in isolation will need more than oxygen and power.

Why a Church Built from Regolith?

The structure in the image is not decoration. Thick sintered or printed Martian soil for radiation shielding, a high-contrast white cross for visibility, and a pressurized interior that can function for a small crew. Representation before permanent settlement.

Low Shame. High Humor. Serious Underneath.

Most people are tired of performative righteousness. On an isolated planet the performance will not survive. The tone of this work is intentional: authentic, a little kickback, and focused on the actual spiritual needs of future settlers.
