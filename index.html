The Future of Faith Off Earth

When people leave this planet and stay, the question of worship does not disappear. Someone has to prepare for it.

If the name made you snort, you're early, not in the wrong place. We heard it too. Then we got to work.

Church of Mars is a non-denominational Christian effort to do that work now. We are not waiting for a permanent habitat to ask what a house of worship looks like on another world. We are starting the conversation, the community, and the plans while we are still here.

Clergy and congregation living far from home will need a sense of humor, starting with us. We are not here to sound holy on a website. We are here to help prepare a spiritual place for people who will live far from Earth.

Conceptual Structure

A compact barrel-vaulted nave, built from Martian soil, with walls thick enough to shield a small congregation and hold the heat. The entrance is an airlock, which is already a kind of church door: you wait, you come through, and the room has to be ready for you. A pure white cross stands in high contrast against the iron-oxide ground and the pinkish sky.

Built from what's already there

Walls 2.5–3.5 meters thick, robotically 3D-printed or sintered from the local soil — in the trade, in-situ resource use, which here just means we do not ship the church from Earth. The print can match the ground around it. Inside: a pressurized liner, so a small group can actually breathe and gather.

The White Cross

A simple white geometric cross on the face of the building, bright enough to read against the rust and the haze. Not a billboard. A marker you can see from a distance, so the place can be found.

Phased Roadmap

1. Conversation

The congregation starts on Earth. Video, dialogue, and community now. Writing and conversation about the future of faith once humans leave this planet.

2. Symbolic

One day we hope to send something small and meaningful, like a Bible, communion elements or microfilm, on an early uncrewed cargo flight to Mars. No flight or provider is arranged. This is an aspiration.

3. Modular

A small chapel with the first crews — built into the habitat, or standing on its own in the local soil. Shielded, pressurized, and modest enough to work for a handful of people. The room may have another job the rest of the sol. That is all right.

4. Congregation

A self-sustaining local church once a permanent settlement exists. Built from the ground up with local materials, and with people who choose to stay. Not a mission outpost that goes home when the contract ends.
