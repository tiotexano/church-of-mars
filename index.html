The Future of Faith Off Earth

When people leave this planet and stay, the question of worship does not disappear. Someone has to prepare for it.

Church of Mars is a non-denominational Christian effort to do that work now. We are not waiting for the first permanent habitat to ask what a house of worship looks like on another world. We are starting the conversation, the community, and the plans while we are still here.

The tone is simple: authentic, low-shame, a little kickback. Clergy and congregation living in isolation will need a sense of humor. We are not interested in performative righteousness. We are interested in the real work of preparing a spiritual place for people who will live far from Earth.

A project of Milky Way Ministries.

Conceptual Structure

A compact barrel-vaulted nave built from Martian regolith. Thick walls for radiation shielding and thermal mass. Partially bermed. Airlock entrance. A pure white cross that stands in high contrast against the iron-oxide ground and pinkish sky.

ISRU Construction

Walls 2.5–3.5 meters thick of robotic 3D-printed sulfur-concrete or sintered regolith. Layered print texture matches the surrounding soil. Pressurized interior with liner. Built from what is already there.

The White Cross

High-contrast pure white geometric cross on the facade. High-albedo material that remains visible against the rusty landscape and hazy sky. Representation that can be seen from a distance.

Phased Roadmap

1. Conversation

The congregation starts on Earth. Video, dialogue, and community now. Content about the future of faith once humans leave this planet. Merchandise that carries the question.

2. Symbolic

Bible, communion elements, or microfilm on an early uncrewed cargo flight. Low mass. First Christian representation on the surface.

3. Modular

Habitat-integrated or standalone regolith chapel with the first crews. Radiation-shielded, pressurized, functional for a small group.

4. Congregation

Self-sustaining local church once permanent settlement exists. Built from the ground up with local materials and people who choose to stay.

The First Congregation Starts on Earth

Before any boots are on Martian soil, the conversation has to exist here. Church of Mars is that conversation — non-denominational, low-shame, and clear that people living in isolation will need more than oxygen and power.

Why a Church Built from Regolith?

The structure in the image is not decoration. Thick sintered or printed Martian soil for radiation shielding, a high-contrast white cross for visibility, and a pressurized interior that can function for a small crew. Representation before permanent settlement.

Low Shame. High Humor. Serious Underneath.

Most people are tired of performative righteousness. On an isolated planet the performance will not survive. The tone of this work is intentional: authentic, a little kickback, and focused on the actual spiritual needs of future settlers.
